OLD TAFO MUNICIPAL ASSEMBLY

Privacy notice.

How the proposed streetlight portal is designed to collect, use and protect information. This draft requires formal OTMA and legal review before production.

Legal alignment note: this draft is structured around Ghana's Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843), but it is not a legal opinion or a confirmation that OTMA has completed any required registration or compliance step. The Assembly should approve the final notice and operational practices before collecting real data.

1. Who is responsible?

The proposed service is operated for the Old Tafo Municipal Assembly (OTMA), Old Tafo, Kumasi, Ashanti Region, Ghana. The public Assembly website lists info@otma.gov.gh and +233 20 394 5459. A designated Data Protection Supervisor and the Assembly's formal data-controller details should be inserted and confirmed before launch.

2. Information the portal may collect

Depending on the final implementation, a fault report may include a reporter's name (optional), phone number, report description, streetlight or map location, nearby landmark, photograph, reference number, date and status history. Staff accounts may include name, work email, role and audit activity. Public statistics should be aggregated and must not display personal reporter details.

3. Purposes and lawful handling

Information should be used only to receive and verify public-lighting faults, contact a reporter when necessary, route work to authorised staff, document inspection and repair, prevent duplicate or fraudulent records, maintain service accountability and produce non-identifying service statistics. OTMA must identify and document an appropriate lawful basis for each processing purpose and explain it at the point of collection.

4. Data protection principles

The design should apply lawfulness and transparency, purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, retention limitation, integrity and confidentiality, and accountability. Access to reporter contact details should be restricted by role. Public reports must not expose private phone numbers, names, photographs containing unnecessary personal details or exact household information.

5. Sharing and service providers

Information may be accessible to authorised OTMA officers and assigned field staff where necessary for the service. Any hosting, mapping, messaging, analytics or cloud provider must be assessed and contractually controlled. Data should not be sold or published for advertising. Cross-border processing and third-party access require specific legal and security review.

6. Security

The production system should use encrypted connections, password hashing, multi-factor authentication for privileged users where feasible, least-privilege access, audit logs, secure file upload validation, backups and a documented incident response process. The portal uses a Supabase-backed reporting and staff data architecture. Actual processing depends on the deployed configuration and the Assembly's approved operating procedures.

7. Retention

OTMA should publish a retention schedule covering report records, photos, communications, staff accounts, audit logs and backups. Retention periods must reflect municipal recordkeeping obligations and the purpose for which information is held. Do not keep identifiable information indefinitely by default.

8. Your rights and requests

Subject to the Act and applicable exceptions, individuals may have rights relating to information about them, including access and correction. Requests should be routed to the Assembly's designated Data Protection Supervisor using verified contact details. The final operational process should explain how requests are authenticated, answered and recorded.

9. Cookies, maps and external services

The portal uses external CDNs for mapping, fonts and charting and OpenStreetMap map tiles. The production implementation should list these services, describe any browser storage or analytics used, and provide appropriate notices and controls. Avoid analytics that collect unnecessary identifiers.

10. Complaints and contact

Contact OTMA at info@otma.gov.gh or +233 20 394 5459. Individuals may also consult the Ghana Data Protection Commission.

Reference: Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843), including sections on data-protection principles, data-controller obligations and rights of data subjects. This page is a product-design draft, not legal advice.
